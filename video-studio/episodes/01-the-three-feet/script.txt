THE THREE FEET

=== 00:00  COLD OPEN ===

There's a feeling you don't have a name for. [.]

It isn't depression. You can still laugh. You can still work.

It shows up at red lights. It shows up when the house gets quiet. [.]

You got some of the things you were supposed to want.
Maybe you got all of them.

And underneath the winning, there's a hum.
Low. Constant. Like something in you is running that shouldn't be. [..]

You tried to fix it with more. More money. More discipline. More plans.
You tried to fix it with less. Fewer feelings. Fewer people. More scrolling. [.]

It's still there in the morning. [..]

Here's what nobody told you.

That hum is not a malfunction. [.]

It's information. [..]


=== 01:00  THE UNREST NAMED ===

You've tried to explain it to someone. [.]
You said you were tired. You said work was a lot.
Both were true. Neither was it. [..]

Here's the closest words get.

You were built to keep things. [.]
And you were handed a world where nothing keeps. [..]

The job keeps for a while. Then it doesn't.
The body keeps for a while. Then it doesn't.
The people you love keep for a while. [..]
And you already know how that sentence ends. [..]

You're not afraid of dying, exactly.
You're afraid that everything you build has a shelf life,
and nobody asked you if that was acceptable. [..]

That's the hum.

It isn't ingratitude. It isn't a chemical error.
It's the part of you that was made for something permanent
running into a world that isn't. [..]

There's a line in an old book.
A man who had all of it — money, buildings, gardens, a reputation —
wrote down what it felt like from the inside. [.]
He said God put eternity in the human heart,
and then wouldn't let us see the whole picture. [..]

Eternity in the heart. No map in the hand. [..]

That's not a design flaw. That's the tension you're standing in.

And it's why getting what you wanted didn't fix it.
You were trying to solve a permanence problem
with a performance solution. [..]


=== 03:15  THE TWO FALSE CURES ===

So a man does one of two things. [..]

The first decides he hasn't won enough.
Earlier mornings. Another income stream. Tighter discipline.
Some of that is good. A man should work. [.]
But he isn't working. He's arguing.
He's trying to prove to something that he isn't a failure. [.]
And you cannot win an argument with a feeling. [..]

The second decides he feels too much.
So he turns it down.
He stops saying what he thinks. He stops asking for anything.
He gets very good at being fine. [..]
He isn't at peace. He's anesthetized.
And the difference shows up at two in the morning. [..]

Both men are doing the same thing.
Trying to make the hum stop
without finding out what it's saying. [..]

And when the present gets unbearable, a man starts living backward. [..]

There's a story about people who got rescued out of slavery.
Real rescue. Real freedom.
A few weeks into the desert, they started talking about the food. [.]
The fish, they said. The melons. The garlic. [..]

They remembered the menu.
They forgot the whip. [..]

That's what your past does. It edits.
It hands you a highlight reel of a life that was killing you
and calls it the good old days. [..]

You're not nostalgic. You're negotiating.
You're trying to make a deal with a room you already walked out of. [..]

And here's the part that costs something.

Some of you aren't looking back at a good thing you lost.
You're looking back at a thing God cut off of you. [.]

And you've been calling the surgery an injury. [BREATH]


=== 05:30  THE LAMP, NOT THE MAP ===

There's a claim coming worth resisting. [.]
No one has to believe it yet. Hold it for a few minutes. [..]

There's an old line about a man walking in the dark.
He says God's word is a lamp to his feet. A light to his path. [..]

Notice what it isn't.
Not a floodlight. Not a map. Not a five-year plan. [..]

A lamp back then was a clay bowl with a wick in oil.
It lit about three feet of road. [.]

Three feet.
Enough for the next step. Not enough for the destination. [..]

And most of what eats at a man isn't the darkness.
It's the demand for more light than he was ever promised. [..]

He wants the whole road before he takes one step.
And he calls that being responsible. [..]

Consider a man who asked God for direction. [.]
He took the job.
He believed he was told to take it.
And he got it. [..]

Then they let him go. [..]

Not for cause. Not for something he did.
Everything within his power, he did.
It was never up to him. [..]

The income stopped. The insurance stopped.
The thing he thought was the answer turned out to be a door. [..]

And he prayed. He didn't scream. He prayed. [..]

Your word says every need will be supplied. [.]
This one isn't. [.]
What's missing? [..]

Now here's what he can't do.

Looking back, he can't prove he was told to take that job.
He believes it. He cannot prove it. [..]

And he has had to make peace with not being able to prove it. [..]

Because the lamp doesn't shine backward.
It never has.
It gives three feet forward.
It does not give an audit of the last turn. [..]

You are not going to get to know. [.]

You're going to get the next step. [..]


=== 08:00  THE THREE UNRESTS ===

That question — what's missing — is the most dangerous question
a man can ask in a hard season. [..]

It assumes the lack is evidence of a defect.
If it isn't working, something must be wrong with him. [..]

There was a man who lost everything. Children. Health. Standing.
Three friends came to explain it to him.
They were certain. They had a system.
Suffering means sin. Find the sin. [..]

He argued with them for thirty-seven chapters. [..]

And when God finally spoke, he never told the man why.
Never explained a single thing that happened to him. [..]

But he turned to the three friends and said his anger burned
against them, because they had not spoken what was right. [..]

The men with the explanation were the ones who got it wrong. [..]

So hold this loosely.
Three ways a man hurts. Not a machine for sorting pain. [..]

THE FIRST.

Something he was told to do that he hasn't done.
He knows what it is. He's known for a while.
He's arranged his life so he doesn't have to look at it. [..]

That's conviction. It doesn't need comfort. It needs obedience. [..]

No amount of waiting on God will fix a thing
a man already knows to do. [..]

THE SECOND.

He's doing right. And it's getting worse. [..]

That's formation. It doesn't need repentance. It needs staying. [..]

THE THIRD.

Nothing is wrong with him.
Nothing is being taught to him. [..]

Someone died. [..]

The world is broken and it hasn't been fixed yet,
and a man who loves anything in it will be torn by that. [..]

There's a line that says the whole creation groans.
Then it says the ones who have the Spirit groan too, inwardly. [.]
Same chapter as everything working together for good.
Twelve verses apart. [..]

The groan is not a failure of faith. It's in the text. [..]

Not everything that hurts is a lesson.
Some things are just the world being what it is until it's made new. [..]

There was a tomb. A man outside it who knew
he was about to open it.
He knew the ending. [.]
He wept anyway. [..]

One question separates the first from the rest.
Is there something already known, already told, that hasn't been done? [..]

If yes — do the thing. [..]

If no, and it's still dark —
stop asking what's missing. [..]

You are not being audited. [.]

You are being kept. [BREATH]


=== 11:00  THE FOUR ROOMS ===

Four rooms. Most men are standing in one of them. [..]

THE FIRST.

He had something he was going to be.
He chased it. It didn't come. So he stopped. [..]

Now he's thirty-eight, and it comes back at strange times.
A song. A name. Someone else doing the thing. [..]

Should have kept going. Should have been braver. [..]

But not every dream laid down is a dream dropped. [.]
Some men put a thing down because something heavier
was already in their hands.
A child. A parent. A bill that wouldn't wait. [..]

That man didn't lose his nerve. He made a trade. [..]

The dream fear took and the dream love cost
look identical at thirty-eight.
They are not the same. [..]

Regret loops. Repentance moves. [.]
If it's the first — get up.
If it's the second — grieve it, and stop calling it failure. [..]

THE SECOND.

He got it. [..]

The title. The salary. The thing he prayed for.
He prayed for it, and it came. [..]

Eighteen months in, and there's a hollow
where the joy was supposed to be.
Nothing wrong. Nothing missing. Just — this isn't it. [..]

And he can't say that out loud. He asked for this. [..]

The dream didn't fail him.
It turned out to be a thing that ends. [..]

You were trying to solve a permanence problem
with a performance solution. [..]

THE THIRD.

He married the woman he loved. [..]

He remembers what he thought it would be.
And somewhere in year seven it stopped being that. [..]

Some of it was him. He knows which parts.
Some of it was life. Her father died.
There was a year neither of them slept. [..]

And he's still there. Every day, still there.
He made a commitment. He's keeping it. [..]

That matters. That's not nothing.
A covenant held in the dark is worth more than a covenant
that was never tested. [..]

But hear the difference.

A man can keep a marriage and lose his wife. [..]

Grit is not love.
Staying is not the same as coming near. [..]

He can hold the line for twenty years
and never once let her see him.
Provide. Protect. Show up. Say nothing. [..]

And she can be lying next to a man she isn't allowed to know. [..]

THE FOURTH.

The number. [..]

He hasn't looked at it in four months.
He knows roughly. Roughly is enough to keep him awake. [..]

Some of it he dug. Twenty-four, stupid, and it was easy. [..]

And some of it fell on him.
A hospital. A year with no income. A child who needed more. [..]

Separate those. [.]
Because a man carries a hospital bill like it's a moral failure,
and it isn't. [..]

The part he dug is a plan, a number, a call he's been avoiding.
The part that fell is grief, not guilt. [..]

And the slowness of climbing out is doing something in him
that getting rich at twenty-four never would have. [..]

THE TURN.

Notice what didn't happen.
None of those got sorted into a box. [..]

Every one has a part he did,
a part that fell on him,
and a part being formed in him. [..]

The work was never labeling the room.
It's separating the three inside it. [..]

You have been carrying all of it as guilt. [.]

Some of it was never yours. [BREATH]


=== 14:45  THE THING CALLED STRENGTH ===

There's a thing men do that gets called strength. [..]

A man decides that because the responsibility is his,
the weight has to be his too.
Nobody else gets to see it. [..]

So he tells his wife the situation.
He doesn't tell her the man inside it.
And he calls that protecting her. [..]

Here's what he gets wrong.

She already knows something is wrong.
She isn't blind. [.]
So she's carrying it too —
she's just carrying it without a name. [..]

And a weight without a name is heavier.
Because now she's filling in the blank herself.
Is it something she did. Is he pulling away.
Is it worse than he's saying. [..]

His silence doesn't reduce her load.
It doubles it. And it takes away her ability to help him carry his. [..]

Now the distinction, because this matters.

Not make a wife a savior.
She can't be that. It will crush her.
Don't hand her the job of making a man okay. [..]

She doesn't have to carry it.
She has to know he's carrying it. [..]

Those are not the same act. [..]

And if there are things she genuinely can't hold —
the answer was never nobody.
The answer is one man who knows the real number. [..]

There's an old line. Bear one another's burdens.
Not bear your own quietly and impress everyone.
Bear one another's. [..]

And when the Son of God entered his worst night,

He asked for company in it.
Then he went a little farther and made the surrender alone. [..]

Both. Not one. [.]

You've been doing the second half

The business is moving. People are responding.
He's getting to the last round. [..]

But be careful there.

Broke, and getting more patient.

The branch that bears fruit gets cut.

The cutting isn't evidence of a wrong turn.

The fruit isn't in his inbox. [.]
