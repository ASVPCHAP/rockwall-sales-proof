# What footage we can use (researched 2026-11-18, not legal advice)

## Bottom line
- **No 1970s–1990s anime is public domain** in the US or Japan, and none will be for decades.
  - Anime made after 1977 is protected for 95 years.
  - Japanese works that lost US protection on a technicality had it restored by the 1996 URAA ([Circular 38b](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ38b.pdf)).
  - Japan protects films for at least 70 years.
- **There's no realistic licence route.** Toei, TMS and Tatsunoko have no public clip-licensing programmes, and Toei enforces aggressively.
- **Fair use doesn't protect motivational narration over unrelated anime.** It draws Content ID claims (the ad revenue goes to the rights holder) and can lead to strikes. BHATT's approach carries that risk.

**Avoid:** *Mr. Bug Goes to Town* (still copyrighted), *Momotarō* films (protected, and wartime propaganda), Masaoka's 1931+ work, Soviet classics (*Hedgehog in the Fog*, *The Snow Queen*).

## Checklist before using any film
1. Published in 1930 or earlier means public domain in the US. 1931–63 US works: check renewals in the Catalog of Copyright Entries or the Copyright Office's records.
2. For foreign works after 1930, assume the 1996 restoration applies.
3. Use old prints, not new restorations, which may carry new rights and Content ID fingerprints.
4. Check the archive's own terms of use.
5. Mute the original audio.
6. Keep character names and logos out of titles and thumbnails.
7. Save the licence page and the search records you used.
8. Upload as **unlisted first** and watch for Content ID claims before going public.
